Multilingual Visualization

Interpretation across linguistic and cultural contexts.

When a chart crosses a language boundary, what exactly has to travel with it?

I study how people interpret, explain, and recreate visualizations across linguistic and cultural contexts. Rather than treating localization as text translation alone, this work examines how users reason about notation, layout, visual convention, and the choices they make when adapting a chart for someone else.

Because apparently charts also have accents.

what I built

A protocol for studying messy human interpretation

I designed a multi-stage visualization study protocol for observing how people move from reading a chart to explaining and redesigning it. The work includes task design, a structured human-coding framework, and component-level evaluation criteria for participant-created visualizations.

Instead of reducing each response to one brittle accuracy score, the evaluation can account for output quality, locale awareness, tool-use strategy, data fidelity, and completeness as distinct dimensions.

why this is hci

Localization is an interaction and design problem

Visualization systems often assume that once text has been translated, the interface has been localized. People are messier than that.

This work studies the interaction between users, visual conventions, and the tools people reach for when something feels unfamiliar. The broader question is how visualization systems should support meaning that depends not only on data, but on the conventions through which that data is presented.

working premise

Localization is not only translation. It is a design decision about what meaning needs to travel.
